# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective clients, collaborators, product teams, and recruiters evaluating Stephen Murya as a product engineer and systems architect. The portfolio should help a visitor understand both the quality of the interface and the thinking, craft, and implementation discipline behind it.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio is a working proof of product engineering: turning ambiguous product and brand problems into considered systems, then carrying those systems through design, implementation, and deployment. It should make it easy to move from a project overview to the underlying decisions, artefacts, and technical details without losing the editorial point of view.

## Brand Personality

Precise, technically credible, and restrained. I work across product design and implementation. I care about the relationship between the interface a user sees and the system that makes it reliable: information architecture, interaction detail, visual language, component structure, performance, accessibility, and deployment. I use tools, including AI, to increase range and speed, while keeping authorship, judgment, and correction in the work.

The voice is first-person, factual, and systems-oriented. It should sound like an engineer who can explain decisions clearly, not like a generic studio or a collection of disconnected visual experiments.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI hype, inflated claims, or copy that implies automation replaced judgment.
- Invented metrics, client outcomes, awards, testimonials, project counts, or technical claims that are not documented.
- Dark-mode SaaS dashboards, glossy agency templates, and portfolio grids that flatten every project into the same thumbnail treatment.
- Pill-heavy controls, rounded-card repetition, decorative gradients, and ornamental UI without a product reason.
- Dense résumé language that hides the work, or vague design language that avoids implementation details.

## Design principles

1. Show the system behind the screen. Projects should connect the brief, decisions, components, implementation, and result.
2. Lead with a clear point of view. The portfolio is a dark editorial personal brand, not a neutral archive.
3. Make the work legible at a glance, then reward depth. Use strong hierarchy, concise metadata, and longer case studies for the reasoning.
4. Keep the interface restrained. Let typography, imagery, spacing, and 1px rules create the structure.
5. Pair ambition with evidence. Explain what was built, how it was built, and what remains unknown.
6. Treat AI as an orchestrated tool in a human-led process. Show where generation accelerated exploration and where skill corrected, composed, and verified the output.
7. Preserve motion as atmosphere, not friction. Transitions should clarify state and support the editorial rhythm.
8. Make the next action obvious: inspect a project, contact me, or continue to the next piece of work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use semantic landmarks and navigation, meaningful heading order, descriptive image alternatives, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus states, sufficient contrast against the black/zinc palette, and reduced-motion behavior. Smooth scrolling, WebGL, and entrance transitions must have a respectful reduced-motion path and must not block access to content or interaction.
